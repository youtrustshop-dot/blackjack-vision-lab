## Continuity behavior and timing

The lifecycle now registers a stable visible deal after a witnessed empty
table even when whose turn it is remains unknown. It can preserve those
exposures without inventing an actionable turn. A stable player-card replacement
without an observable boundary and with unknown phase becomes an ambiguous
history: commits pause, earlier exposures are not rewritten, and a later clear
may establish new identities while the earlier history gap remains explicit.

`temporal_evidence` exposes separate lifecycle hits/requirement, commit permission,
tracker hits/label-confirmation counters, missing tracks, phase and turn
provenance. In the pixel-only unit fixture with both windows set to three,
observations 1–2 await the lifecycle; 3–4 await the tracker; first face-up commits
occur at observation 5. At that fixture's 200 ms cadence this is 800 ms after
the first input, excluding real capture/network/process delays. This explains
why three total observations can lose exposures. The windows were not lowered.
It is a unit fixture result, not a new provider/video benchmark or a universal
explanation of every 0/4/4 historical error.

Unit contracts separately cover witnessed clear, immediate settled hands,
unknown initial deal, unobservable replacement, and recovery without falsely
repairing lost history. The real-video runner isolates labels from inference,
rejects reused runs/hashes and changed code, retains short decisions in the
denominator, checks per-rank inventory despite equal Hi-Lo sums, and never
duplicates decoded frames to satisfy confirmation.
